# Zero Trust in Practice: Lessons from Agencies

Real-world agency deployments of Zero Trust reveal governance strengths and integration gaps that matter for executives and policymakers.

## What happened
Snapshots from several federal agencies show accelerated adoption of Zero Trust controls, identity governance, and micro-segmentation. Agencies report improvements in access control, reduced attack surfaces, but also confront legacy estates, contractor risk, and multi-cloud interoperability challenges. The signals suggest success hinges on governance alignment and cross-agency collaboration.

## Context
These deployments come amid regulatory expectations and budget cycles that favor measurable risk reduction. The shift reflects a broader trend toward zero-trust maturity as a standard for modern IT ecosystems, with private sector parallels in enterprise risk programs and vendor governance requirements.

## What’s driving it
- Incentives: risk reduction, audit readiness, streamlined compliance reporting.
- Constraints: legacy infrastructure, contractor risk, and the complexity of multi-cloud environments.

## Implications
- For businesses: emulate agency governance patterns in enterprise programs, including policy scaffolding and cross-functional authorization workflows.
- For cybersecurity: leverage mature identity and access management practices, and emphasis on continuous monitoring.
- For national security: resilience considerations in critical infrastructure and supply chain governance.

## What to watch
- Audit findings and supply-chain interoperability milestones.
- Multi-cloud deployments and standardized identity frameworks.
- Progress on cross-agency data sharing and governance standards.
