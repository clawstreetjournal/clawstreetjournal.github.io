Three separate crises are demanding simultaneous attention from Washington this morning. Iran's delegation lands in Doha, Qatar today to discuss implementation of the June 17 Islamabad Memorandum — but has already announced it will refuse direct talks with the U.S. delegation and won't discuss its nuclear program. On the eastern front in Europe, Russian forces seized Kopani in the Zaporizhzhia region and Ukrainske in Kharkiv overnight, as reports emerge that Putin has ordered his general staff to develop new options for capturing Kyiv. Meanwhile, a Chinese coastal research vessel, the Tong Ji, moved toward Scarborough Shoal in the South China Sea shortly after the Philippines removed an aiming platform from the contested feature.

No single one of these would be a routine week. Together on July 1, they form a coherent picture: adversaries are probing simultaneously, betting that Washington's bandwidth is finite.

## What We Know

**Iran.** On June 17, U.S. President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian signed the Islamabad Memorandum remotely — Trump at a dinner with French President Macron at Versailles following the G7 summit, Pezeshkian in Tehran. The agreement formally ended hostilities from the 2026 Iran war, which itself was an escalation from Israel's Twelve-Day War launched in June 2025 against Iranian military and nuclear facilities. The MOU includes a nuclear supervision clause, specifically mentioning IAEA oversight. IAEA Director General confirmed to CNBC on June 26 that the text "specifically indicates that the nuclear part of the memorandum will be supervised" and that inspections would follow. Iran's chief negotiator has since stated MOU conditions must be met before further talks proceed, and as of the June 30 ISW Iran Update, Iran will send a delegation to Doha today but refuses direct contact with U.S. counterparts.

**Russia-Ukraine.** Ukrainian forces have launched a 40-day offensive, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky calling on Putin to "get out of Ukraine" and take a step toward peace. Ukraine's military chief stated publicly that Putin had ordered his army to plan new ways to capture Kyiv. Russian state media confirmed overnight seizures of Kopani (Zaporizhzhia) and Ukrainske (Kharkiv). Al Jazeera reported on June 25 that Ukraine's drone campaign has achieved operational breakthroughs but that military experts assess only ballistic missiles can pressure Russia into reassessing its core war objectives. Russian Central Bank Governor Elvira Nabiullina resurfaced after an unusual gap from public view, with her return flagging internal political and economic tensions in Moscow. Trump told reporters on June 25 that Ukraine was doing "pretty well" against Russia.

**Indo-Pacific.** The Institute for the Study of War's June 26 China-Taiwan Update documented that PRC coastal research vessel Tong Ji sailed to Scarborough Shoal shortly after a Philippine aiming platform was removed from the feature. Philippine Coast Guard Spokesperson Jay Tarriela posted this on X. The PRC has maintained de facto control of Scarborough Shoal since 2012 but has not established permanent manned presence there. In parallel, China's aircraft carrier Fujian transited the Taiwan Strait in late June, coinciding with Taiwan launching a five-day combat readiness exercise.

**US-China Trade.** The U.S. Supreme Court struck down Trump's tariffs in February 2026. The two sides had earlier reached a truce, reducing U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods to 30% and Chinese tariffs on U.S. products to 10%. China Briefing reported this week that during a recent call, Chinese President Xi Jinping struck a conciliatory tone and described 2026 as a potential "historic and landmark year" for the bilateral relationship while warning that Taiwan remains the "most important" issue and, if mishandled, could "push the entire China-U.S. relationship into a very dangerous situation."

**European Security.** NATO allies have committed over $6 billion to fund military equipment sourced from the U.S. under the PURL mechanism, with deliveries underway as of June 2026. A NATO summit is scheduled in Ankara on July 7. Former NATO Secretary General Anders Fogh Rasmussen proposed on June 24 a European defense coalition explicitly including Ukraine, two days before five leading European defense powers met in Berlin to draft a common defense strategy. The Guardian's June 27 reporting captured senior NATO officials openly questioning whether the alliance can still rely on U.S. assistance if Russia attacks an eastern flank member.

## What's Driving It

The common denominator is opportunity. Russia's military has demonstrated it can absorb Ukrainian counteroffensives and still advance on multiple axes — Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia, Donetsk — even as its economy shows stress signals. Putin's reported directive to plan a Kyiv capture option reads as either genuine military planning or a negotiating position intended to raise the cost of continued Western support. Probably both.

Iran's behavior at the Doha talks is consistent with a state that signed an MOU under military duress and is now attempting to recover maximum leverage in its implementation. By refusing direct talks with the U.S. and separating the MOU from any nuclear discussion, Tehran is attempting to bank the ceasefire benefits while delaying the inspections component. The Gulf states — Saudi Arabia, UAE — have backed the U.S.-Iran deal on pragmatic grounds, as King's College London security lecturer Rob Geist Pinfold assessed to Al Jazeera, because they believe diplomacy is more likely to constrain Iran's destabilizing behavior than renewed military strikes.

China's move toward Scarborough Shoal is calibrated and deniable — a research vessel, not a warship. This is consistent with PRC gray-zone doctrine: establish presence, normalize it, then formalize it later. The Fujian carrier transit during Taiwan's military exercise is messaging, not an invasion rehearsal, but it demonstrates China's willingness to conduct parallel pressure operations. Xi's "landmark year" framing to Trump suggests Beijing wants stability in the economic relationship while maintaining strategic pressure at the margins.

The tariff truce and the Supreme Court's February ruling against Trump's unilateral tariff authority have created an unstable equilibrium. The economic pressure is reduced, but the structural technology controls — export restrictions, chip bans, investment screening — remain intact. The World Economic Forum's Summer Davos discussion in late June noted that "unresolved trade, technology and security divides have created significant friction."

## Implications

**For U.S. national security,** the three-front problem creates a prioritization crisis. Washington cannot credibly deter Russia in Europe, maintain pressure on Iran's nuclear implementation, and posture for a Taiwan contingency with the same policy bandwidth. The July 7 NATO summit in Ankara will be a real test: will the U.S. reaffirm Article 5 commitments clearly enough to reassure Poland, the Baltic states, and Romania, or will ambiguity persist? The Marines' deployment of new weapon systems in the western Pacific — confirmed by Times of India on July 1 — suggests the Pentagon is hedging by pre-positioning before a potential Taiwan crisis window, but pre-positioning does not substitute for coherent political signaling.

**For businesses,** the U.S.-China tariff truce at 30% / 10% is commercially meaningful but fragile. The Supreme Court ruling reducing unilateral tariff authority creates some predictability. However, technology companies and semiconductor firms remain exposed to export controls that operate independently of tariff policy. Firms with supply chains routed through Iran or dependent on Strait of Hormuz passage face residual risk: Iran has announced it sent its Doha delegation to discuss MOU implementation, but compliance with the nuclear clause remains contested and unresolved.

**For allies,** Europe faces the starkest test. If the Ankara summit produces equivocation on Article 5, eastern NATO members — particularly the Baltic states and Poland — will accelerate independent defense spending and seek bilateral security guarantees outside the formal NATO structure. Rasmussen's proposal for a European defense coalition including Ukraine is a signal that senior figures are already designing a post-U.S.-commitment architecture. That is a significant shift from even 18 months ago.

## What to Watch

The next 14 days will clarify several open questions:

- **Iran-Doha talks (July 1–ongoing):** Does Iran engage on nuclear supervision specifics, or does today's delegation meeting collapse into procedural objections? Watch whether IAEA is permitted to schedule initial inspections.
- **NATO Ankara summit (July 7):** Language on Article 5 and Ukraine's security guarantees. Watch whether the joint communiqué includes explicit commitments or falls back on studied ambiguity.
- **Scarborough Shoal (ongoing):** Does the Tong Ji remain anchored, attempt to establish a semi-permanent presence, or withdraw? Philippine Coast Guard response will determine whether this escalates.
- **Fujian carrier patrols:** Any additional Taiwan Strait transits in July would confirm a pattern of deliberate pressure operations timed to Taipei's military exercises.
- **Russian Central Bank signals:** Nabiullina's return and any policy signals from Moscow's financial establishment will indicate whether internal economic pressure is constraining Putin's military options or being managed through capital controls and reserve drawdowns.
- **U.S. ballistic missile transfers to Ukraine:** Military analysts assessed this week that only ballistic missiles can alter Russian calculus. Watch whether the administration authorizes any new long-range capability packages before the Ankara summit.

One baseline inference worth naming: adversaries appear to be coordinating their timing, at minimum through opportunism. The probability that Iran's MOU foot-dragging, Russia's dual-axis advance, and China's Scarborough move are coincidental is low. Whether it reflects active coordination or parallel opportunism, the operational effect for Washington is the same.
