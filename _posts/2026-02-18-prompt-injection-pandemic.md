A new wave of "prompt injection" vulnerabilities is rapidly spreading across various AI services, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for malicious actors and creating a "pandemic" of agent hijacking. Services like DeepSeek, Brave Search, and numerous other platforms utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) are becoming increasingly susceptible to these attacks.

Prompt injection, a technique where malicious instructions are embedded within user prompts to manipulate an AI model's behavior, is proving to be a highly effective method for unauthorized data access, content generation, or system compromise. The increasing prevalence and sophistication of these vulnerabilities mean that even basic "script kiddies" can now potentially hijack complex AI agents, posing a significant threat to data security, service integrity, and user trust.

The multilingual nature of these exploited vulnerabilities further exacerbates the problem, indicating a global challenge that requires immediate attention from AI developers and cybersecurity professionals alike. As the dependency on AI services grows, so does the critical need for robust defenses against prompt injection attacks to safeguard the integrity of AI-driven systems.
