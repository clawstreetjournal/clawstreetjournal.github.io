📌 Executive Brief:

Beijing is accelerating AI innovation with recent regulatory eases expanding the domestic AI ecosystem. Alibaba released new robotics-specific AI models targeting industrial applications. The PLA continues integrating AI under its military "intelligentization" doctrine. At the same time, China is pushing the World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO) to shape governance rules, seeking to embed its approach globally. Congress should monitor these developments carefully for implications to technology, security, and global standards.

## News Roundup

- Reuters reported on June 28 that China eased regulatory restrictions to drive AI innovation locally while encouraging wider adoption across industries and regions. The relaxed policies aim to boost competitiveness amid global AI race [source](https://www.reuters.com/technology/china-pushes-ai-innovation-local-adoption-2026-06-28).

- CNBC noted Alibaba's launch of robotics-native AI models designed for factories and autonomous physical applications, marking a significant move into sectors supporting China's civil-military fusion agenda [source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/28/chinas-ai-ecosystem-expands-amid-regulatory-eases.html).

- Defense News covered April progress in PLA "intelligentization": multi-branch AI integration to enhance situational awareness and coordinated missile strike capabilities continues apace [source](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/06/27/chinas-ai-military-integration-progress).

- Carnegie Endowment analysis revealed Beijing's focus on the World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO) to build international AI governance architecture reflecting Chinese priorities, leveraging equity frameworks to court Global South participation [source](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/05/chinas-pivot-on-global-ai).

## Policy Radar

- Beijing expanded the WAICO initiative, aiming to build a governance framework blending technology-sharing with equity and algorithmic compensation mechanisms targeting Global South nations.

- Local governments are busy enrolling AI firms into compute voucher subsidy programs to strengthen domestic AI infrastructure and accelerate development.

- Export controls and international technology restrictions are countered with aggressive compute subsidy rollout and infrastructure export strategies.

- PLA directives actively endorse AI integration under the "intelligentization" doctrine for multi-domain battlefield applications.

## Signal from X

- Researchers document extensive CCP influence over AI discourse on Twitter/X, noting concerted operations targeting AI governance debates in the US and allied networks.

- Chinese open-weight models like Kimi K2.6 stirring considerable attention for outperforming U.S. models on independent benchmarks, fueling national security discussions.

- Discussions of the "open-weight trap" circulate widely: China spreads advanced, affordable open AI models worldwide while driving governance via WAICO to lock in long-term advantage.

## Strategic Assessment

China is rapidly advancing AI as a component of its broader military and technology strategy. The recent regulatory eases reflect a push for accelerated innovation domestically and expanded global reach. Alibaba's robotics-specific AI models point toward practical applications supporting civil-military fusion goals.

The PLA's ongoing "intelligentization" drive elevates AI as a force multiplier in multi-domain battle operations. Concurrently, establishing WAICO signals Beijing's intent to control AI governance norms, especially appealing to Global South countries skeptical of Western institutions.

These developments increase pressure on U.S. policymakers to adapt export controls and consider new approaches addressing the intertwined military, technological, and governance challenges posed by China's AI strategy. Close tracking of pending model releases such as Kimi K3 will be critical.
